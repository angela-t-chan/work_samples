Remote CPR Guidance — Multimodal Feedback for Untrained Bystanders

A real-time guidance system connecting untrained bystanders to medical dispatch operators during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. Working directly with the founder from 0→1, I led research and interaction design to determine how haptic, visual, and voice feedback channels can be combined to convey compression force and rhythm to a panicked, non-expert user — without overwhelming them. The core research challenge was not technical feasibility but perceptual legibility under stress.

Research focus Multimodal channel design, force perception under cognitive load
Methods Interaction design, Wizard-of-Oz, prototype evaluation
Role Lead researcher & designer (founding team)

Tactile teleoperation Wearable systems $1.5M funded Texas A&M × Houston Methodist

Haptic Telemedicine Glove — Remote Palpation for Neck & Thyroid Examination

Led end-to-end formative research for a wearable haptic glove that transmits tactile sensation from an on-site nurse to a remote specialist during neck and thyroid exams. Before any hardware was built, the project required defining what the system needed to do perceptually, not just technically — characterizing clinician behaviors through contextual observation, video task analysis, and expert interviews, then translating findings into interaction requirements and a minimum viable fidelity specification. Research secured $1.5M in development funding and was published in Sensors (2025).

Research focus Tactile fidelity thresholds, clinical workflow, interaction requirements
Methods Contextual observation, video task analysis, hierarchical task analysis, stakeholder interviews
Outcome $1.5M development funding · Published, Sensors 2025
2017 – 2024
Haptic communication Social computing Best Paper · HCII 2024 NSF-funded

Mediated Touch Communication — Haptic Systems for Emotional Connection Across Distance

A multi-year NSF-funded research program studying how wearable and haptic technologies can support emotional connection and social presence between people who are physically separated. Projects spanned the design and evaluation of novel interaction paradigms — including asynchronous touch messaging between long-distance couples and the physiological effects of co-verbal remote touch — using multimodal data collection integrating EDA, HRV, facial expression analysis, and behavioral logging.

Research focus Emotional response, social presence, touch-mediated communication
Methods Controlled lab studies, physiological sensing (EDA/HRV), sentiment analysis, behavioral logging
Outcome 4 peer-reviewed publications · Best Paper, HCII 2024 · 500+ attendees

Selected publications

2025

Sensors

Design and Development of Remote Sensors for Medical Neck and Thyroid Palpation

Chan, A., Kim, N., Kawazoe, A., Friesen, R., Ferris, T., and C. Hipwell

Presents the design and development of wearable remote sensor systems for haptic transmission during clinical neck and thyroid examination. Grounded in formative research characterizing clinician palpation behaviors and workflow constraints, the work defines interaction requirements and validates technical feasibility for a $1.5M-funded telemedicine glove prototype developed in partnership with Texas A&M University and Houston Methodist Hospital.

Sensors Special Issue: Wearable Technologies and Sensors for Healthcare and Wellbeing

2024

HCI International

Closing the Gap Between Long Distance Couples via Asynchronous Mediated Touch Communication

Chan, A., Quek, F., Howell, J., and T. Liu

Investigates how asynchronous haptic messaging can support emotional intimacy and social presence between geographically separated couples. The study examines interaction patterns, perceived closeness, and the design requirements for touch-mediated communication systems that operate outside of real-time synchrony constraints. Presented to 500+ global attendees at HCI International 2024.

★ Best Paper Award · HCII 2024

2021

Sensors, 21(1)

The Effect of Co-Verbal Remote Touch on Electrodermal Activity and Emotional Response in Dyadic Discourse

Chan, A., Quek, F., Panchal, H., Howell, J., Yamauchi, T., & J.H. Seo

A controlled study examining how haptic touch delivered during remote conversation affects electrodermal activity (EDA) and self-reported emotional response in dyadic pairs. Findings characterize the physiological and affective signatures of co-verbal touch, with implications for the design of emotionally responsive haptic communication systems.

04 — About

I'm an HCI research scientist with a PhD from Texas A&M, where I spent seven years studying how physical sensation can be transmitted, translated, and acted on across distance. My work spans haptic telemedicine, emergency guidance systems, and social touch — connected by a common question: what does it take for a remote system to feel real enough to matter?

I work at the design-before-build phase, where research questions and product decisions are still the same question. That means partnering closely with engineers and clinicians to surface what's perceptually and clinically true before hardware or software is committed — and building the cross-functional alignment needed to move from insight to implementation.

I'm currently available for senior applied research roles and R&D collaborations in industry.
