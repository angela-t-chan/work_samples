Real-Time CPR Guidance — Designing Feedback for High-Stress Emergency Action

A two-month 0→1 product design project for a real-time CPR guidance platform. I helped shape the early product experience for two intended users: a bystander performing chest compressions and a remote medical expert guiding them in the moment. The work focused on making the system simple enough to use under stress — clarifying what feedback the bystander should receive, what information the expert needed to see, and what belonged in the MVP versus later versions.

Research focus Actionable feedback under stress, multimodal cueing, expert monitoring needs
Methods 0→1 product framing, interaction prototyping, founder alignment

Haptic Telemedicine Glove — Translating Clinical Touch into System Requirements

A telemedicine research project exploring how tactile information from an in-person neck and thyroid exam could be captured and communicated to a remote physician. I analyzed clinical training videos, characterized key palpation motions and pressure levels, and helped translate those observations into sensor placement and feedback requirements for an early haptic prototype. The work made an ambiguous clinical interaction more concrete for engineering: what the system needed to detect, what level of tactile fidelity mattered, and where remote examination workflows would constrain the design.

Research focus Clinical behavior, palpation gestures, sensor and feedback requirements
Methods Training video analysis, ENT specialist interview, requirements synthesis
Outcome MVP & Prototype requirements · Published, Sensors 2025

Mediated Touch Communication — Evaluating How Haptics Change Remote Interaction

A multi-year NSF-funded research program studying how haptic feedback can support emotional connection across distance. I designed and evaluated interaction models across asynchronous media messaging, music sharing, and live video conversations, combining interviews, observations, interaction logs, self-reports, and physiological signals such as EDA, HRV, and PPG. Across studies, the research clarified how timing, intensity, personalization, and context shape whether touch cues feel supportive, meaningful, or distracting in remote interaction.

Research focus Social presence, affective/emotional response, remote communication/interaction behavior
Methods Experimentation/Controlled studies, physiological sensing, interviews, interaction logging
Outcome Peer-reviewed publications · Best Paper, HCII 2024

Design and Development of Remote Sensors for Medical Neck and Thyroid Palpation

Chan, A., Kim, N., Kawazoe, A., Friesen, R., Ferris, T., and C. Hipwell

Characterizes clinician palpation behaviors and translates those findings into design requirements for wearable remote sensing during neck and thyroid examination. The work connects clinical workflow, tactile interaction, and prototype feasibility for a telemedicine system developed through Texas A&M University and Houston Methodist Hospital collaboration.

2024

HCI International

Closing the Gap Between Long Distance Couples via Asynchronous Mediated Touch Communication

Chan, A., Quek, F., Howell, J., and T. Liu

Investigates how asynchronous haptic messaging can support emotional intimacy and social presence between geographically separated couples. The study identifies interaction patterns and design requirements for touch-mediated systems that operate outside real-time synchrony constraints.

2021

Sensors, 21(1)

The Effect of Co-Verbal Remote Touch on Electrodermal Activity and Emotional Response in Dyadic Discourse

Chan, A., Quek, F., Panchal, H., Howell, J., Yamauchi, T., & J.H. Seo

A controlled study examining how haptic touch delivered during remote conversation affects electrodermal activity and self-reported emotional response in dyadic pairs. Findings clarify how touch cues can shape affective experience in remote communication systems.

04 — About
