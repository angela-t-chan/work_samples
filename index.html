The Problem
350K+
out-of-hospital cardiac arrests each year — where bystander CPR can double or triple survival chances

How might we help an untrained bystander perform effective CPR while waiting for EMS, when verbal coaching alone may fail under pressure?

  • CPR quality depends on both rhythm and force-related movement.
  • Rhythm can be coached verbally, but force-related movement needs a more tangible feedback model.
  • The platform needed to translate physical action into guidance that helps experts monitor and bystanders act.
System Context
Two Users, One Real-Time Feedback Loop

The platform connects a remote medical expert and a bystander through one feedback loop. It receives compression movement data from a connected wearable and sends pacing cues back to the bystander.

Medical Expert / Dispatch Side
  • Monitors target vs. responder compressions
  • Tracks compression quality over time
  • Provides verbal feedback and guidance
Bystander Side
  • Follows target compression signal
  • Receives pacing cues
  • Sees real-time correction feedback
  • Receives verbal guidance from expert/dispatch

Design Decisions
Decisions That Turned Ambiguity into Product Direction

Each card shows the tradeoff, the design decision, and the product impact.

Rhythm can be coached. Force has to be translated.
The Core Challenge
  • Tradeoff: Rhythm could be coached verbally, but force-related movement was harder to explain.
  • Decision: Treat force as a feedback problem, not just an instruction problem.
  • Impact: Reframed the product around translating physical action into guidance.
Guidance had to reduce cognitive load, not add to it.
Advocating for the Bystander
  • Tradeoff: Verbal-only coaching required the bystander to listen, interpret, and act at the same time.
  • Decision: Distribute guidance across pacing cues, visual feedback, and voice.
  • Impact: Shifted the product from “better instructions” toward real-time physical guidance.
One signal, two dimensions of feedback.
Interface Tradeoff
  • Tradeoff: A color ring could show force, but rhythm still required a second signal.
  • Decision: Use a scrolling waveform where height represents force and spacing represents rhythm.
  • Impact: Reduced split attention by giving users one continuous signal to follow.
Validate the feedback loop before adding complexity.
Sequencing Decision
  • Tradeoff: Expert and bystander views would eventually need different levels of detail.
  • Decision: Start with a shared waveform model to validate the core feedback loop first.
  • Impact: Protected the first version from premature complexity.
